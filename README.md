# Global CO2 Inequality: Findings and Recommendations
Armando Dominguez, Jiewen Huang, Kevin Elshemy, Hawa Kora

## Client
**The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC)**
- In 2015, 196 nations negotiated the Paris Climate Accords
- All parties set their own goals to limit the global rise in surface temperature to less than 2°C.

## Agenda
- Context
- Executive Summary
- Findings
- Recommendations

## Context
- **The Problem**: Global carbon emissions have risen dramatically due to industrialization, transportaion, and energy production.
- **The Importance**: The global economy could lose 10% of its total economic value due to climate change and emissions can push the planet beyond the self-imposed 1.5 - 2°C warming threshold.
- **Attempts to Fix This**: The 2015 Paris Agreemnent and the 2021 Glasgow Climate Pact.

## Executive Summary & Recommendations
**Summary**
- High-income countries have historically accounted for the largest share of CO2 emissions globally. However, over the past decade and a half, upper-middle-income countries have overtaken upper-income countries in this regard.
- Low-income countries account for less than 3% of global carbon emissions, with their modest increases over the years driven mainly by population growth and expanding access to basic energy as opposed to heavy industry.
- Europe's per capita emissions is nearly seven times higher than Africa's, highlighting a global imbalance where developed regions contribute disproportionately more to climate change despite smaller populations.
- Both South America and Oceania emitted a long term increase in annual Carbon Dioxide (CO2) emissions. However, South America consistently emits more carbon than Oceania.

**Recommendations**
- Adopt a global carbon benchmark (4.5 tons per capita) with a scaled fine for any country with emissions tht surpass the benchmark.
- Redirect carbon tax revenues fron high-income nations towards renewable energy, clean technology, and infrastructure in low-income countries; ensuring climate fairness while supporting sustainable growth.
- Invest in regional green infrastructure to balance economic growth with emissions reduction.
- Incentivize the further development of cleaner, more energy-efficient technologies by the higher-income countries for eventual adoption by lower-income countries.
